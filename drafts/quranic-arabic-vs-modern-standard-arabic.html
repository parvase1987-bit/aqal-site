Quranic Arabic vs Modern Standard Arabic: which should you learn?

If your goal is to understand the Quran, learn Quranic Arabic: its grammar, practiced on Ayat, with the words the Quran uses most. Modern Standard Arabic shares most of that grammar, so nothing is wasted if you learn it too, but a general course spends much of its time on modern words and everyday topics you won't meet in the Quran.

What the two names mean

  • Quranic Arabic is the Arabic of the Quran. It belongs to Classical Arabic, the written language of the Quran, the Hadith and early Arabic writing.
  • Modern Standard Arabic (MSA) grew out of Classical Arabic and is used today in newspapers, books, the news on TV and formal speeches across the Arab world.
  • Spoken dialects (Egyptian, Levantine, Gulf, Moroccan and others) are what people speak at home and in the street. They differ from both of the above much more than the two differ from each other.

What is the same, and what differs

Quranic ArabicModern Standard Arabic
GrammarThe same core: the three kinds of word, word endings that show a word's role, verb patterns built on roots.The same core. Some constructions are more common in one than the other, and many MSA speakers drop the word endings when they read aloud.
WordsThe Quran's own vocabulary, including words that are rare today.Many modern words (computer, government, election) and modern meanings for old words.
StyleRhythm, repetition, changes of speaker, compressed phrasing.The style of news reports, essays and official writing.
Where you meet itThe Quran, prayer, Islamic texts.News, books, signs, formal talk.

Which one to pick

  • You want to understand what you recite and hear in prayer: start with Quranic Arabic. You see results in the Ayat you already know.
  • You want to read the news, travel or talk with Arabic speakers: learn MSA, and a dialect for speaking.
  • You want both: either order works, because the grammar carries over. If you start with the Quran, most of what you add for MSA later is vocabulary.

A note on "Quranic Arabic" courses

Some courses called Quranic Arabic are vocabulary courses: they teach the meanings of frequent words. That helps, and it is quick. But without grammar you can know every word in an Ayah and still not see who is doing what, or to whom. Look for a course that teaches both, and practices on real Ayat rather than made-up sentences.
