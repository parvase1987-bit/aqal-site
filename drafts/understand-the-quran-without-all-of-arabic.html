Can you understand the Quran without learning all of Arabic?

Yes, to a real degree. You don't need to speak Arabic or know modern vocabulary to follow the words of an Ayah. You need three things: the three kinds of word, the grammar that shows each word's role, and the words the Quran repeats most. Going deeper into what an Ayah means is the work of tafsir, and still needs scholars.

What "understand" can mean

There are levels, and it helps to be clear which one you are aiming for:

  1. Know what the words say. You hear an Ayah and recognize most of its words and who is speaking to whom.
  2. See how the words fit together. You can tell which word is doing the action, which one receives it, and what a small change in an ending does.
  3. Explain the meaning and its rulings. This is tafsir. It takes years of study and is not something an app, or this page, gives you.

Levels 1 and 2 are within reach of anyone who can read the script and practices a little every day.

Every word is one of three kinds

English has around eight word types. Arabic has three: Ism (names or describes), Fi'l (an action with time built in) and Harf (connects words). Here are the opening words of Al-'Ankabut 29:44, each colored by its kind:

خَلَقَ اللَّهُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ بِ‍‍الْحَقِّ

"Allah created the heavens and the earth in truth." Al-'Ankabut 29:44

Fi'l · Ism · Harf

Once you can sort a line like this, grammar has something to hang on: endings belong to an Ism, tenses belong to a Fi'l, and a Harf is often only a letter joined to the front of the next word.

Learning words like these first means you recognize part of almost every Ayah early on. Reviewing them just before you would forget them (after 3, 7, 21, 60 and 120 days in Aqal) keeps them.

What you can skip, at least at first

  • Speaking and listening practice for conversations.
  • Modern vocabulary: phones, offices, politics.
  • A spoken dialect.

None of these hurt, but none of them are needed to follow an Ayah. If you later want them, the grammar you learned from the Quran still applies.
